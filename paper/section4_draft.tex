\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Baseline Portfolio}

We establish the difficulty of the benchmark against a strong portfolio of
automated off-the-shelf provers. Because the task is two-sided, the portfolio
must be as well: proving a law trivial requires proof search, while finding a
nontrivial model requires a saturation or completion that terminates. The
portfolio spans eight configurations — Vampire~5.0.1 in five modes (proof
search and saturation, under both Knuth-Bendix and lexicographic path
orderings) \citep{kovacs2013first}, the E prover in a proof and a saturation
mode \citep{schulz2019faster}, and Twee~2.6.1, an implementation of unfailing
completion built for equational reasoning \citep{smallbone2021twee} — each run
over a ladder of per-prover budgets of 30, 60, 120, 300, and 600 seconds. A
law counts as resolved as soon as any configuration returns a verdict, and a
claimed model or triviality proof is one the tool itself reports rather than
one we infer. Provers, versions, and exact flags are pinned in a released
container image. We deliberately omit finite-model finders such as Mace4 and
Paradox: admissibility certifies that no nontrivial finite model exists, so
these tools cannot succeed by theorem. Saturation and completion are the
automated methods able to attempt infinite-model construction at all.

The \emph{hard tier} is the set of admissible laws that no configuration of
this portfolio resolves. The screening pass of the previous section runs a
single prover at the shortest budget; the full portfolio then sweeps its
residual, so the tier is defined against every prover, ordering, and budget we
have, not against any one tool.

\subsection{The Method-Bound Frontier}

The screening prover resolves the easy mass of the pool cheaply, building all
262 Austin models in a median of 0.1 seconds each. Sweeping the 4,141-law
residual with the full portfolio recovers a thin layer the short pass missed:

absorbs it — every such law simply moves to its proper tier. What remains

Its difficulty is bound by method rather than budget, and Table~\ref{tab:curve}
shows the evidence. If compute were the limit, resolution would climb as the
budget grows. Instead, on a 120-law sample re-attacked with all eight
configurations, three laws resolve at the 30-second rung — all trivial — and
none at 60, 120, 300, or 600 seconds, roughly $170\times$ the per-law time of
the screening pass. A separate retry of 294 unresolved laws at ten times the
screening budget converts 3.7\%, all to trivial, and none to a model.
Resolutions arrive cheaply or never: models are found in fractions of a
second when completion terminates, and no additional budget makes it
terminate. The residual is stable under more time and more provers; what it
resists is the methods themselves.

Nor is the difficulty one prover's blind spot. Vampire and E search by
superposition, deriving consequences until a contradiction or closure; Twee
runs unfailing completion, orienting the law into rewrite rules. The two
paradigms explore the space differently enough that each resolves instances
the other diverges on — Twee completes in seconds an order-five law whose
saturation runs Vampire to hundreds of clauses, and the sweep's [FILL: 4]
recovered models are completion-only. Yet the paradigms' reach ends in the
same place: past the shed contamination, neither constructs a model the other
misses, and the residual resists both. The hard frontier is a property of the
laws, not of a tool.

\subsection{LLM Baseline}

Current language models enter ALPS with the same standing as the portfolio:
nothing in the task references how an answer is produced, only that it
certifies. We evaluate them under a protocol designed so that a failure is
attributable and a zero is meaningful. Every model receives identical inputs
— the law, the task statement, and a fixed answer format — and interacts with
the judge as a verification oracle over a fixed number of feedback rounds.
On the trivial side we remove the formalization burden entirely: the model
emits only a chain of terms, each step one application of the law, and the
harness matches every step, fills short gaps, and assembles the Lean proof
itself, so the test is finding the derivation rather than writing Lean.
Support is graded: runs range from the bare law to full reveal, where the
prover-derived waypoint lemmas of the reference refutation are included in
the prompt. Saturation provers are withheld from the models — with them, a
model would simply re-run the baseline at larger budget — while the judge and
the assembler are exactly what any solver built on ALPS would use.

Instance selection is the part we control most carefully. From the trivial
tier we isolate a certified-easy set: laws whose complete collapse chains a
blind bidirectional search finds in under five seconds, most only three
applications long. Each such law carries a privately held, machine-found
solution, and one of them, driven through the assembler and the Lean kernel,
serves as an end-to-end positive control. A zero on this set cannot be
blamed on the instances: the answers exist, are short, and are found by
uninformed search almost instantly.

\subsection{Case Study: an Order-5 Law Left Open by ETP}

% Positioning guardrails: resolved by the AUTOMATED pipeline; NOT hard-tier;
% "an order-5 law and its dual" (ONE case up to duality), never "two problems".

The Equational Theories Project's order-five chapter lists the laws it proved
to admit only trivial finite models but whose infinite-model existence it
left unresolved. Two of them — law 12857,
$x = y \op ((x \op (y \op (z \op z))) \op y)$, and its mirror image 33436 —
sit in our pool, and our pipeline settles them: one open case, up to duality.
Both are confirmed Austin by two independent routes, a Vampire saturation and
a Twee completion, and the extracted model is exercised directly — every rule
of the 27-rule system fires, and $x = y$ is refuted in it. The case is a
demonstration that the pipeline produces mathematics on literature-open
questions, not a claim against the baseline: the portfolio itself resolves
these laws. What the example also shows is why the construction side is hard
to formalize — the completed system is not orientable into a plain rewrite
system, which is precisely the ground-confluence obstacle the verification
section defers to companion work.
